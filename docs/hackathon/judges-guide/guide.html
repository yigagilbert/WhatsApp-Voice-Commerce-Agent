JUDGE'S GUIDE

Run a small shop from WhatsApp.The AI prepares. Code handles the money.

Katale AI turns WhatsApp text and voice notes into priced orders, PayPal payment links, stock updates and business insight. The AI only proposes: the backend calculates every price, and payment requests need a typed one-time code from the merchant.

Test it in about 2 minutes

Sign in at kataleai.space with the sample login credentials in the testing instructions section of the project details on Devpost. The Agent console runs the same AI pipeline as WhatsApp, in your browser. Steps 7 and 8 use WhatsApp itself.

  1. Agent console: send Which products are running low? The answer comes from live stock records.
  2. Agent console: send Create an order for Sarah for 2 handmade baskets. The backend prices a draft, and nothing reaches PayPal until an owner types the one-time code.
  3. Products: click a stock count, change it and press Enter. This is AG Grid inline editing with server validation.
  4. Overview: click the How is this week going? chip, then type Build me a chart of net revenue by channel. AG Studio adds the chart to an unsaved AI dashboard tab.
  5. Overview, Products & inventory tab: right-click a bar in Product revenue and choose See the records behind this.
  6. Orders: open an order, then View the full trace to follow message, AI decision, approval and PayPal.
  7. WhatsApp as a customer (no setup): open kataleai.space/chat and send hi. Pick a product, then ask for a better price.
  8. WhatsApp as the merchant (optional): open Admins, add your number in international format (for example +256700000000), choose Owner to try payment approval, and press Send test. Then try a question, an order or an English voice note.

Why WhatsApp

Small sellers in Africa already sell in chats, so Katale asks for no new app and no new habit. In a GeoPoll survey (July 2025; 3,945 mobile users aged 18–50 in four African countries), 90% used WhatsApp and 64% used it for business. The sample skews younger and more affluent. The shop also has a public page, with no login, whose link can go in a social media bio. Its cart continues in WhatsApp.

How a payment stays safe

1. AI preparesUnderstands text and voice, then picks a typed tool.
2. Backend verifiesCustomer, product, price, stock and totals from the database.
3. Merchant approvesA one-time code bound to that one order. A spoken "confirm" is refused.
4. PayPal executesCheckout, capture and signature-verified webhooks.

How bargaining stays safe

Customers can haggle on WhatsApp, as they would at the market. For each product, the merchant can set a private lowest price. The AI chooses how to say an offer, never the number: the backend calculates each one, and the AI never sees the minimum. A price guard checks every amount in an AI reply before it is sent, and the agreed price is what goes to PayPal.

How we use PayPal

Orders v2 Creates the checkout link for each approved order and captures the payment.
Webhooks Verified by signature and idempotent. They mark orders paid, record refunds and raise dispute alerts.
Order tracking Sends carrier and tracking numbers when the merchant ships. Built, not shown in the video.
Disputes v1 Lists cases, then submits the merchant's response after a typed code. Built, not shown in the video.
Reconciliation Checks open payment links against PayPal.

How we use AG Grid and AG Studio

AG Grid Orders, products, payments, customers and disputes grids, with filters, sorting, CSV export and inline editing of price and stock.
AG Studio The Overview dashboard: report pages, filters and right-click drill-through.
AG Studio AI The assistant answers from deterministic lookups and adds charts from a checked list of metrics. Nothing is kept until the merchant saves it.

Stack

Next.js and Tailwind dashboard; Fastify API; PostgreSQL with Prisma; whatsapp-web.js worker; an OpenAI-compatible LLM (gpt-4.1-mini) with typed tools; OpenAI Whisper for voice notes; Docker deployment.

Known limits

  • Where it works: any WhatsApp number, but receiving money needs a PayPal business account that can receive payments in the seller's country. PayPal doesn't support UGX, KES, TZS or RWF, so prices are in USD or another PayPal currency.
  • WhatsApp connection: for now we use whatsapp-web.js, an unofficial client that WhatsApp's terms don't allow and that can get a number blocked. We plan to move to the official WhatsApp Business API soon after company registration.
  • Voice: English is the supported language. The voice note in the video was a test take with a synthetic voice.
  • Accounts: one merchant per installation, with no self-serve multi-merchant sign-up yet.
